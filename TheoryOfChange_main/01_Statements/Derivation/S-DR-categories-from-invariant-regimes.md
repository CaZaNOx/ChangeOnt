# Categories from invariant regimes — token, pattern, boundary, role, position
## Claim (formal)
Distinct ontological categories are derived from distinct invariant regimes rather than from primitive substance-kinds.

## Philosophical Translation (of formal claim)
What we call objects, information, passwords, roles, or status are not basic ingredients of reality. They are different ways change stabilizes under different preservation rules.

## Philosophical Justification
Once invariant regimes are explicit, category differences can be explained without smuggling in static kinds. [[S-DF-embedded-stabilized-layers]] and [[S-DF-border-localization]] add that these categories are always encountered from inside layered inherited collapses and bounded local holds on change-space. A token-like entity is one whose regime preserves bearer-history tightly; an information-like pattern is one whose regime preserves form across many carriers; a password-like case is a pattern regime embedded in gating and functional closure; status-like cases preserve comparative position in a field.

## Explanation (informal)
This derivation is the theory-side bridge to the kernel map. It explains why later primitives/elements can be grouped by the closure patterns they operationalize instead of by everyday example words.

## Derivation (Philosophical)
- If identity depends on invariants, then different invariant regimes yield different kinds of persistence.
- Narrow bearer-binding yields token continuity.
- Wide form-preservation across carriers yields pattern equivalence.
- Boundary-sensitive closure yields gating/boundary regimes.
- Functional continuity yields role regimes.
- Comparative field-preservation yields positional regimes.
- Therefore categories are derived from preserved aspects and tolerated transformations as encountered by bounded embedded structures.

## Derivation (Formal/Logical/Mathematical)
```text
Given regimes R_token, R_pattern, R_boundary, R_function, R_position,
Category(P) is determined by the dominant preserved aspect I_R and closure condition C_R.
```

## Clarifications / Further Context
- Real cases are often composite and may sit near borders between regimes.
- Embedded stabilized layers explain why many problem-spaces arrive already heavily collapsed before local reasoning begins.
- This file does not replace detailed kernel derivation; it supplies the missing theory bridge that makes kernel mapping intelligible.
