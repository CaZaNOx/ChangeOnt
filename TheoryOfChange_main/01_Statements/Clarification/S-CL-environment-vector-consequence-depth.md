# Consequence depth states how far beyond local evidence the true consequence of present choice can lie
## Content
Consequence depth measures how far beyond currently salient local evidence the decisive consequences of present choice can lie. This is not the same as local-progress reliability: local cues may be individually sound yet still require long unfolding before their full significance becomes visible.

## Why this vector is needed
The kernel must know when present choice remains shallowly resolvable and when deep path consequences remain live. Otherwise it may collapse at the wrong horizon.

## Kernel relationship
High consequence depth legitimizes longer-path unfolding, delays hard collapse from short-horizon evidence, and raises the value of deeper relational tracing before lock-in. Low consequence depth allows more immediate closure from local support.

## Placement guidance
Ask: How many steps, updates, or relational moves can lie between a current decision and its decisive consequences? How often do shallow judgments get overturned only at greater path depth?

## Directional law
Increasing delayed reward, longer tactical traps, deeper search trees, or long-horizon consequences should raise this vector. Making the task shallow and immediately revealing should lower it.

## Misuse warning
This vector is global only if it is measured by the same admissible logic across problems. It must not be replaced by family labels or by hidden oracle information. If a problem makes this vector hard to estimate, the right output is a wider region or lower confidence—not a family-specific guess.
