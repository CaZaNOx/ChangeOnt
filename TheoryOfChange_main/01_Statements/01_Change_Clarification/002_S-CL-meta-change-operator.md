# Meta-change is recursive self-modulation, not a second ontology

## Claim (formal)
Meta-change is not an additional primitive over and above change. It is change altering its own mode of variation, retention, propagation, or organization.

## Philosophical Translation (of formal claim)
When the theory speaks of “change of change,” it should not be heard as introducing a second metaphysical ingredient. It should be heard as saying that the primitive is not frozen into one single manner of unfolding. Change can alter not only local contents, but also how change itself proceeds.

## Philosophical Justification
If change were primitive only in the sense of “some content changes,” but the *mode* of change were itself eternally fixed, then the ontology would be too weak to explain:
- regime shift,
- operator drift,
- novelty in the manner of unfolding,
- or the emergence of new organizational constraints.

A primitive that cannot in any way alter its own pattern of operation becomes strangely passive. Yet CO aims to avoid both:
- passive monism,
- and a dualism in which a second principle must be added to make the primitive dynamic enough.

Meta-change is the middle path: the primitive remains one, but that one primitive can vary both local differentiations and its own mode of varying.

## Explanation (informal)
At a simple level:
- ordinary change = what is happening changes,
- meta-change = how the happening proceeds also changes.

That is enough for the present file. No second ontology is required.

## Derivation (Philosophical)
- CO takes change to be primitive.
- A primitive locked into one invariant mode of operation cannot explain shifts in manner, regime, or generative profile.
- Introducing a second principle to explain such shifts would weaken the monism.
- Therefore the more economical and stronger move is to allow change to modify its own mode of operation.
- That is what is meant by meta-change.

## Derivation (Formal/Logical/Mathematical)
```text
ordinary change: x -> x'
meta-change: the rule or mode by which x -> x' also drifts
```
This is schematic only. The point is recursion of modulation, not a second substance.

## Clarifications / Further Context
- Meta-change is not mystical “super-change.”
- It is not a second layer floating above reality.
- It remains internal to one ontology.
- It should not be invoked lazily whenever the theory is unclear; each later use should specify what is changing about change.

## Objections addressed

### Objection 1: “Is this not just a second hidden primitive?”
No. A second primitive would introduce a new ontological category. Meta-change does not do that. It says only that one primitive is capable of self-modulation.

### Objection 2: “Why not just say ‘process changes’?”
That may be acceptable if “process” means nothing thicker than unfolding happening. But where “process” already carries more structure, the present clarification remains more basic.

### Objection 3: “Does this make change too vague?”
Not if used correctly. Vagueness appears when meta-change is invoked without specifying what feature of unfolding is drifting. The right use is narrow: change can alter its own operative profile.

## What this file establishes
This file establishes that CO may legitimately speak of “change of change” without abandoning monism.

## What this file does not yet establish
It does **not** yet establish:
- any particular operator,
- any particular regime taxonomy,
- or any detailed formal dynamics.

It establishes only the conceptual legitimacy of recursive self-modulation.
