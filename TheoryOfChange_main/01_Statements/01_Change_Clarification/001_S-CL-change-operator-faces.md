# The family-name “change” and its faces: processive happening is closest at the root, richer articulations come later

## Claim
The project keeps **change** as its broad family-name, but the opening must distinguish different true faces of that family. At the root, the closest lower-bound articulation is **processive happening / unfolding**. Later files may earn richer senses — structured change, operative change, regime-sensitive change — but may not silently import them back into the root.

## Why the family-name is retained
The project is not trying to rename itself every time a new aspect is clarified. “Change” names the broader ontological family because later articulations really are articulations of one and the same underlying reality.

## Why processive happening is closest at the root
At the opening, the safest articulation is not yet:
- object A changing into object B,
- state-transition in a defined space,
- or process in a thick technical sense.

The safest articulation is simply that there is live taking-place: happening, occurring, unfolding.

## Practical rule for later files
When a later file says “change,” it must make clear whether it means:
- root processive happening,
- structural unfolding,
- or later operative articulation.

Without that discipline, the chain becomes vulnerable to equivocation.

## What this establishes
1. “Change” remains the broad family-name of the project.
2. “Processive happening / unfolding” is the closest lower-bound articulation at the root.
3. Later files may introduce richer faces of change, but only explicitly.

## What this does not yet establish
It does **not** yet establish:
- that all later articulations are already earned,
- or that one single exclusive lexical choice must replace every other true aspect-description.
