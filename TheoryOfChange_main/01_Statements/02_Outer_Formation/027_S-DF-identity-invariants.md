# Identity invariants — preserved content within recognized continuity
## Claim (formal)
Identity invariants are the preserved aspects of a bounded continuing line that remain stable across recognized admissible transformation and thereby support later re-identification and stabilization.

## Philosophical Translation (of formal claim)
Once continuity has been recognized as identity-bearing, we can finally ask what content is actually being carried through the transformation. Those preserved aspects are the identity invariants.

## Philosophical Justification
[[01_Statements/02_Outer_Formation/011_S-DF-invariant-regime]] gives the general idea that some aspects of unfolding remain preserved across tolerated transformation classes. [[01_Statements/02_Outer_Formation/026_S-DF-identity-recognition-structure]] gives the narrower recognition side of identity. Only then can the chain ask what content remains preserved within that recognized continuity. Identity invariants are therefore not the recognition machinery itself but the preserved content it makes available for later re-identification.

## Explanation (informal)
This node answers: granted that a continuing line has been recognized, what preserved content makes that line answerable to the same bounded continuation-profile strongly enough for identity to persist?

## Derivation (Philosophical)
- Invariant regimes provide the general preserved-versus-altered idea.
- Recognition structure determines when continuity is treated as one bounded continuing line.
- Within recognized admissible continuity, preserved content can be extracted.
- That preserved content constitutes identity invariants.

## Derivation (Formal/Logical/Mathematical)
```text
Given recognized admissible transformation T over bounded process P,
Inv_id(P,T) = those preserved aspects of P that remain stable enough across T to support later ReID and stabilization.
```

## Clarifications / Further Context
- Invariants are not identical with similarity, admissibility, or threshold.
- Threshold sharpens recognition; invariants state what content remains preserved once recognition is in place.
- This node is narrower than general invariant regime and belongs specifically to the identity branch.
