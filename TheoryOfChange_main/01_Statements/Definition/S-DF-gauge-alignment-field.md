# Gauge / Alignment field
## Claim (formal)
Once comparison is local and frame-sensitive, a transport/alignment field is required to carry comparison judgments coherently across changing local frames. Gauge names this transport/alignment field before any heavier geometric formalism is imposed.

## Philosophical Translation (of formal claim)
If different local views of unfolding do not automatically line up, then a system needs a way to transport or align what counts as comparable from one frame to another. Gauge is that alignment requirement.

## Philosophical Justification
[[S-DF-border-localization]] states that local structures do not capture total change-space and therefore compare from bounded standpoints. [[S-DF-local-comparability-field]] then yields local graded comparison, preservation burden, or admissibility cost. Once these judgments are local, shifting frames threaten incoherence unless some transport/alignment condition is available. Gauge is first that transport/alignment necessity; invariants are one downstream beneficiary, not the primitive reason gauge exists.

## Explanation (informal)
Gauge is not first a borrowed connection/curvature package. It is the simpler ontological need to keep local comparison judgments coherent when the local frame changes.

## Derivation (Philosophical)
- Comparison is local rather than globally absolute.
- Local frames of unfolding can shift, reweight, or expose different aspects.
- Therefore a transport/alignment relation is needed to compare comparisons across frames.
- Gauge names that transport/alignment field.

## Derivation (Formal/Logical/Mathematical)
```text
local comparability + frame shift -> need to transport/align local comparison judgments
-> gauge/alignment field
```

## Clarifications / Further Context
- Gauge is conceptually prior to full connection/curvature language.
- The field should be read as transport/alignment first, heavier geometry later.
- Identity invariants and later kernel primitives use gauge, but do not justify its first appearance.
