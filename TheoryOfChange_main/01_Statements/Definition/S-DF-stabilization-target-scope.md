# Stabilization-target scope — anchor, hypothesis, and emergent profile must not be conflated
## Claim (formal)
A runtime stabilization process must distinguish at least three scopes of what may be stabilized: (i) regime-given anchors, (ii) hypotheses over such anchors, and (iii) emergent continuation-profiles. A single undifferentiated support or hardening law must not silently treat these as the same kind of target.

## Philosophical Translation (of formal claim)
Not everything that feels stable is stable in the same way. Sometimes the environment already gives the identity and only the latent significance is uncertain. Sometimes the identity itself is what is still emerging. Those cases cannot be handled honestly by pretending support is always doing one and the same job.

## Philosophical Justification
[[01_Statements/Definition/S-DF-regime-shape-variation]] already establishes that local unfolding differs in how it tightens, loosens, or reopens. [[01_Statements/Definition/S-DF-hdr-common]] then turns that into a live collapse/reopening estimate. [[01_Statements/Definition/S-DF-self-propagating-selective-retention]] shows that what is carried forward is selective rather than total. [[01_Statements/02_Outer_Formation/027_S-DF-identity-invariants]] shows that identity depends on preserved content within recognized continuity rather than on mere recurrence. Together these imply a scope distinction. Where the regime already fixes the relevant slot or anchor, support should not pretend it is discovering that anchor's identity. Where the anchor is fixed but its latent role is uncertain, support properly matures rival hypotheses. Where no such anchor is already given, support may instead act on an emergent continuation-profile. Treating all three as one ladder would collapse distinct ontological situations into one runtime convenience.

## Explanation (informal)
Bandit-like settings make the distinction visible. The arm identities are already given by the regime. What remains uncertain is which arm is best. So support there should harden value hypotheses, not promote the chosen arm into earned identity privilege. In transformed-object or attractor-like settings, by contrast, the continuation-profile itself may be what must earn identity-status through accumulated support.

## Derivation (Philosophical)
- Regimes differ in how much structure is already inherited and stabilized.
- Selective retention and identity invariants show that not all carried structure has the same role.
- Some local structures are already fixed enough to count as anchors before current local evidence arrives.
- Uncertainty can therefore fall either on the anchor's latent role or on the emergence of a continuation-profile itself.
- Therefore a stabilization process must distinguish stabilization scope rather than applying one undifferentiated hardening story everywhere.

## Derivation (Formal/Logical/Mathematical)
```text
stabilization_target ∈ {anchor, hypothesis(anchor), emergent_profile}

If target = anchor:
  support may bind evidence to an already-given slot but must not pretend it discovered the slot.
If target = hypothesis(anchor):
  support matures rival beliefs over a fixed slot.
If target = emergent_profile:
  support contributes to whether a continuation-profile counts as the same line under change.
```

## Clarifications / Further Context
- This file justifies the need for a scope distinction. It does **not** yet derive one unique scoring law.
- The present trichotomy is a minimal bridge taxonomy, not a claim that no richer taxonomy will ever be needed.
- Regime-given anchors can still be uncertain in observability or access; the point is that their identity is not being earned by current local support.
- This file therefore licenses a support layer only in a qualified sense: support is conceptually needed, but its exact law remains provisional and scope-sensitive.
