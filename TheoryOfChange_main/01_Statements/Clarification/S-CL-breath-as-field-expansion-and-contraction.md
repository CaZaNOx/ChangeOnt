# Breath as field expansion and contraction, not primitive ontology

## Claim
Breath is not a primitive ontology term and not a mystical force. In the operational chain, breath names a derived regulation pattern: alternation between expansion and contraction of live continuation structure under pressure.

## Expansion
Expansion occurs when the system must keep more structure live:

- preserve grey alternatives;
- widen branch/relation visibility;
- recurse into unresolved pressure;
- retain non-equivalent paths;
- delay collapse because operative difference remains.

## Contraction
Contraction occurs when the system may lawfully thin:

- quotient equivalent paths;
- cancel neutralized branches;
- collapse nonoperative grey;
- commit to an action expression;
- transmit a thinner summary.

## Relation to spiral analogy
A circle over-preserves recurrence without operative novelty. A line over-diverges without retained reference. Spiral-like behavior is a local attractor-pattern in which recurrence and deformation remain coupled.

This does not make “spiral” the ontology. Change remains primary. Spiral and breath are derived patterns that may appear when change retains enough reference while deforming.

## Kernel interpretation
For RCF:

```text
expansion = preserve live non-equivalent branches and relation topology
contraction = quotient / cancel / earned collapse
```

A runtime should not import the word “breath” as an unexplained mechanism. It may implement expansion/contraction controls if those controls are grounded in branch pressure, path density, relation topology, and earned-collapse doctrine.
