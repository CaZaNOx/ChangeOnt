# Primitive family — Change ops (bounded relational motif calculus)
## Claim (formal)
ChangeOps is the broader family of relational operators over bounded change-space: detect, compare, compose, split, quotient, and reuse motifs of change without privileging absolute scale.

## Philosophical Translation (of formal claim)
Small moves make bigger moves, but what matters is not absolute size. What matters is relational structure inside a bounded local frame.

## Philosophical Justification
Eventlets and paths give local atoms and order. Closure/quotient gives tolerated sameness. [[S-DF-border-localization]] clarifies that every local hold on change-space is bounded. Therefore the native operator family should be relational and local rather than absolute and globally metric in the classical sense. This family is broader than the final kernel subset because a bounded relational space may support more lawful operations than the kernel can presently carry.

## Explanation (informal)
The circle example makes the point: two differently sized bodies can still enact the same relational operation inside their own bounded frame. ChangeOps therefore tracks relational transformation families rather than absolute magnitudes.

## Derivation (Philosophical)
- Eventlets supply local change atoms.
- Paths supply ordered local chains.
- Bounded localization blocks naive absolute treatment as fundamental.
- Therefore a broader relational family of change operators is legitimate.

## Derivation (Formal/Logical/Mathematical)
```text
Given bounded local motifs M,
allow relational operations over M that preserve declared quotient and closure relations.
```

## Clarifications / Further Context
- This is the broader family, not yet the compact kernel library.
- Later memory, compression, and grouping mechanisms naturally depend on this family because bounded local structures must filter and boil down rich unfolding.
