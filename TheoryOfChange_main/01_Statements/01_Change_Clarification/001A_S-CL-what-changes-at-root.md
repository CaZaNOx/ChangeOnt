# What changes at the root is not a pre-given thing but the live articulation of happening itself

## Claim
At the root of the chain, what changes is not yet a finished bearer, object, or substance. What changes first is the **live articulation of happening itself**: its moving boundary, contour, and unfolding differentiation.

## Why this clarification is needed
The familiar question “what is it that changes?” usually assumes that change must be parasitic on a prior thing. That assumption is exactly what the opening is trying not to grant.

The root does not first give us a thing plus a later modification. It gives us a taking-place whose articulation is not flat. So the earliest answer to “what changes?” is:
- the live articulation/boundary of happening itself,
not yet
- a stable bearer carrying an added property of change.

## Moving-boundary clarification
A useful picture here is not sealed point-events but a moving, refinable boundary. What later cognition may pick out as “event A” and “event B” are coarse-grained stabilizations cut from an underlying unfolding. The change lives first in the ongoing differentiation itself, not only in the already-frozen relata.

## Why this matters against substance-first views
If one says that existence or substance is primary, then change must later be introduced as another primitive fact or as an unexplained activation. This creates either:
- a duality of static base + active change,
- or a hidden second principle.

If one starts from happening/change instead, then later stability can be treated as a derivative articulation within the unfolding. That is more parsimonious.

## Argument
1. The opening secures occurring before stable objecthood.
2. Therefore the chain is not yet entitled to say that a finished thing stands beneath the change.
3. What is first available is the non-flat articulation of the occurring itself.
4. Therefore the earliest answer to “what changes?” is: the articulation/boundary/contour of happening itself changes.
5. Stable bearers may later be derived as thicker formations within that unfolding.

## Objection 1: “A changing articulation is still just another thing.”
Not in the strong substance-like sense being resisted here. The present file is not introducing a hidden bearer but only naming the fact that happening is already articulated, and that this articulation does not remain fixed.

## Objection 2: “Isn’t this relation-first rather than change-first?”
The two are very close here. The intended point is that the changing lives in the moving boundary or relation rather than in sealed finished relata. The project can therefore reasonably be read as relation-first in this thin root sense, so long as the relation is understood as lived unfolding rather than a static formal link.

## What this file establishes
- the chain need not begin from pre-given entities,
- the earliest changing is the changing of articulation itself,
- later bearer-like stability must be derived rather than assumed.

## What this file does not yet establish
- the full later ontology of objects,
- or the final theory of stability.
