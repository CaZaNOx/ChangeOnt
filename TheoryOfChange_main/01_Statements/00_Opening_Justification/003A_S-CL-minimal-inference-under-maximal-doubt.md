# Maximal doubt cannot import a finished logic, but it also cannot dissolve inquiry into noise

## Claim
Maximal doubt does not entitle the opening to borrow a completed logical architecture as already given. But it also does not permit total inferential collapse. If inquiry is still being enacted, then some minimal distinction-discipline remains in play.

## Why this file is needed
A recurrent objection runs in two opposite directions:

- **Too much:** if the opening infers anything at all, then it has already smuggled in a full prior logic.
- **Too little:** if the opening refuses all inferential structure, then it cannot even formulate doubt, retention, or exclusion.

Both objections are too crude. The real question is not whether there is *any* structure at the opening, but whether the opening is importing **thick, unearned structure** rather than retaining only the thinnest discipline required for inquiry to remain enactable and determinate.

## The middle position
The opening does **not** yet assume:
- a full formal calculus,
- a completed theory of truth,
- all the laws of classical logic in finished form,
- or a complete mathematics.

It does retain enough to distinguish:
- this from not-this,
- retention from cancellation,
- continuation from collapse,
- and enacted consistency from enacted failure.

Without at least that much, there would be no difference between inquiry and non-inquiry.

## Why this is not illicit smuggling
Every enacted inquiry already has some structure. So the real contrast is not:
- “no structure” versus “full logic.”

It is:
- **minimal enacted distinction-discipline** versus **thick imported architecture**.

The opening keeps the former and suspends the latter.

## Argument
1. Maximal doubt is still an enacted inquiry rather than blank erasure.
2. Any enacted inquiry must preserve enough distinction for there to be this inquiry rather than total collapse.
3. Therefore maximal doubt cannot abolish every inferential distinction.
4. But maximal doubt also refuses to grant a completed prior logic, because that would import more structure than has yet been earned.
5. So the correct opening position is a middle one: retain only the weakest distinction-discipline required for inquiry to continue as inquiry.

## Why this matters for ontology
The purpose of this middle position is not to win a technical logic dispute. It is to prevent a bad dilemma at the opening:
- either smuggle in a whole prior logic,
- or destroy the very inquiry that is meant to test the ontology.

A foundational chain that destroys its own ability to distinguish, retain, and test cannot say anything at all about ontology. So the minimum distinction-discipline is not a luxury; it is the price of having any opening route whatsoever.

## What this establishes
1. Maximal doubt cannot proceed in total inferential collapse.
2. The opening may use minimal distinction-discipline without borrowing a full prior logic.
3. That is enough for the next opening steps.

## What this does not yet establish
It does **not** yet establish:
- a completed logical calculus,
- classical logic as final,
- or the later CO-logic developments.
