# Burden as anchored operative tension

## Claim
Burden is anchored operative tension: residual transformation pressure carried by a continuation relative to a condition of admissible continuation.

This file further sharpens the earlier formulation by adding the *field-asymmetry / de-centering* account:

```text
ongoing change-field
→ retained local structure
→ asymmetry / deformation around that structure
→ de-centering relative to a continuation or closure condition
→ tension
→ operative tension under an anchor
→ burden
→ directed pressure / transformation demand
```

Change itself is not derived from tension. Change is the primitive. Tension explains why local change becomes directional, pressured, or burdened once a retained structure is situated in an asymmetrical field of unfolding.

## Philosophical translation
A retained structure does not remain stable by escaping change. It remains stable by continuously handling the de-centering produced by ongoing surrounding change.

If the surrounding field is locally symmetric relative to that structure, no direction of unfolding is privileged. Tensions cancel or remain nonoperative. If the field is asymmetrically deformed, the structure is no longer centered relative to its own continuation condition. That de-centering is tension. When the tension matters for whether the structure can continue, stabilize, transform, quotient, or collapse, it becomes burden.

```text
stable ≠ unchanged
stable = able to absorb, route, or re-center tension without identity-breaking burden
```

This is why burden is not generic cost, badness, difficulty, or uncertainty. Cost, difficulty, or uncertainty may measure burden only when they track the residual transformation pressure relevant to continuation.

## Relation to existing burden definition
`S-DF-remaining-transformation-burden` establishes burden as what still has to change for a present unfolding to reach a supportable continuation. This derivation refines the source and structure of that burden:

- tension is relational pressure generated by difference in ongoing change;
- asymmetry/de-centering gives tension direction;
- a continuation anchor selects which tension is operative;
- burden is the residual transformation requirement that remains operative under that anchor.

So burden is not generic cost, badness, uncertainty, or distance. Cost, uncertainty, or distance may measure aspects of burden only when they track residual transformation pressure relative to continuation admissibility.

## Definitions

### Change-field
*Change-field* := the surrounding relational unfolding within which a retained local structure is situated.

The term does not imply a literal physical space. It names the locally relevant field of possible and actual relations, pressures, admissibilities, closures, and transformations.

### Retained local structure
*Retained local structure* := a persistence-through-change sufficient to be answerable as the same local unfolding across a span.

Examples include an organismic form, a candidate continuation, a route, a machine-state continuation, a chemical closure, or a subject-like salience pattern. None is outside change; each persists by retaining enough structure through change.

### Field asymmetry
*Field asymmetry* := unequal deformation of the local change-field such that possible continuations are no longer equivalent.

When field asymmetry is absent or nonoperative, local tensions cancel or do not privilege a direction. When asymmetry becomes operative, a direction of pressure appears.

### De-centering
*De-centering* := the displacement of a retained structure from the local continuation, stability, or closure condition under which it remains supportable.

De-centering is not necessarily spatial. It may be ecological, logical, semantic, energetic, evidential, topological, or operational.

### Tension
*Tension* := relational pressure generated by field asymmetry / de-centering under ongoing change.

Tension may be background, latent, nonoperative, balanced, local, field-level, or identity-relevant. It does not by itself count as burden.

### Continuation anchor
*Continuation anchor* := the condition under which a local unfolding is answerable as supportable, admissible, or relevant.

Examples:

- maintain machine function;
- reach a maze goal;
- reduce uncertainty enough for admissible commitment;
- preserve identity-through-change;
- keep a route open;
- preserve a chemical closure;
- remain viable under co-changing ecological relations;
- stabilize a subject-pole or body-relevant salience field in later consciousness work.

### Burden
*Burden* := anchored operative tension; the residual transformation pressure carried by a continuation because it is de-centered relative to a condition of admissible continuation.

A burden is therefore relational. It is always burden-for a continuation under an anchor, not an isolated substance.

### Burden type
*Burden type* := an equivalence class of anchored operative tension identified by:

1. the continuation or closure condition it pressures;
2. the class of transformations that can relieve, cancel, expose, transfer, transform, absorb, or buffer it;
3. the local comparison regime under which those operations remain comparable.

A burden type is not primarily identified by source. Different sources may produce the same burden type; one source may produce several burden types.

## Symmetry, direction, and pressure
A local field may contain tension without directed pressure if the tension is symmetric or canceling.

```text
symmetric field around retained structure
→ no privileged direction
→ no directed burden
```

A field asymmetry breaks equivalence among possible continuations.

```text
asymmetrically deformed field
→ local de-centering
→ direction of pressure
→ burden if continuation-relevant
```

This is the conceptual reason burden cannot be represented only as a scalar magnitude. A complete burden account must distinguish:

- magnitude: how much operative tension;
- direction: what kind of transformation would reduce/express/escape it;
- barrier: how accessible that transformation is;
- basin: whether the structure is stable, metastable, unstable, overclosed, or underclosed;
- coupling: whether the tension actually affects the continuation anchor.

## Same-type criterion
Two burden tokens count as the same type when:

```text
1. they pressure the same continuation condition or same class of admissibility/closure condition;
2. they are comparable under the same local burden operation;
3. the same class of transformation could alter their burden-status;
4. treating them as same-type does not erase an operative difference for relation, quotient, recursion, or collapse.
```

They differ in type when relieving, exposing, canceling, absorbing, or transforming one does not address the continuation pressure of the other.

## Source is not identity
Source is metadata, not burden identity.

Examples:

- wear, random shock, and overuse may all create degradation burden if the same repair/reset transformations address the same pressured machine-continuation;
- one blocked door may create topological obstruction burden, hiddenness burden, and revision burden;
- a misleading reward signal may mask degradation burden while creating later revision burden;
- one extra particle/electron/mutation may be nearly irrelevant or decisive depending on closure, coupling, barrier, and scale.

The same quantity of change can behave differently because burden depends on relational position in the field, not amount alone.

## Scale, coupling, and threshold
Not all field tension becomes burden. Tension becomes burden only when it survives scale-filtering and couples to an active continuation/closure condition.

### Scale-filtering
Many micro-tensions may average out at a larger scale. They become burden only if their aggregate or threshold behavior changes continuation.

### Coupling
A tension matters only when it couples to the relevant anchor. A small perturbation may be decisive if it couples to a closure condition, while a large perturbation may be irrelevant if it does not.

### Threshold / phase-shift
Burden may remain low until a closure, barrier, phase, or admissibility threshold is crossed. Then a small change can reorganize the burden regime.

This explains why stability is not absence of change. Stability is the capacity to absorb or route ordinary tensions without converting them into identity-breaking burden.

## Tension becoming operative
Tension becomes burden only when it changes at least one of:

- continuation admissibility;
- carried burden of a branch;
- relation topology;
- quotient/equivalence status;
- grey-preservation need;
- recursion demand;
- collapse-readiness;
- downstream commitment conditions.

This prevents the field from exploding into total holism. CO is relational, but the kernel tracks operative relations, not every possible relation.

## Burden operations
The minimal burden operations are:

```text
carry
amplify
expose
mask/postpone
relieve
cancel
transfer
transform
absorb/buffer
threshold/phase-shift
```

These are not yet numerical laws. They are operation kinds over anchored operative tension.

### Carry
A continuation keeps a burden live without resolving it.

### Amplify
A continuation increases same-type operative tension.

### Expose
A transformation makes hidden or latent burden available to the field.

### Mask / postpone
A transformation reduces apparent urgency or visibility while leaving operative burden unresolved.

### Relieve
A transformation reduces a burden while preserving the relevant continuation condition.

### Cancel
A transformation removes or resets the condition under which a burden remains active.

### Transfer
A burden changes carrier or scope while preserving type.

### Transform
A burden changes type because the unresolved requirement changes form.

### Absorb / buffer
A structure takes up or routes incoming tension without converting it into operative burden at the relevant scale.

### Threshold / phase-shift
A burden regime changes discontinuously because a closure, barrier, or coupling threshold has been crossed.

## Relation derivation principle
Branch relations should be derived from burden operations, not chosen as arbitrary labels.

Examples:

```text
A carries burden type X.
B relieves burden type X.
→ relief(B, A)

A carries burden condition X.
B cancels or resets condition X.
→ cancellation(B, A)

A and B cannot both preserve their pressured conditions.
→ rivalry(A, B)

A and B have same burden profile under active tolerance.
→ quotient(A, B)

A buffers tension that would otherwise become burden for B.
→ shielding/buffering proximity(A, B)

A crosses a threshold that changes B's burden regime.
→ phase-shift / critical-proximity relation(A, B)
```

## Consequences for kernel architecture
The adapter may expose public facts that indicate possible burden effects. The kernel must project those facts against the continuation anchor and derive burden-status, burden operations, and branch relations.

Allowed adapter output:

```text
this transition can reduce degradation
this observation can reveal hiddenness
this move is blocked by public topology
this interaction can reset condition X
this process can buffer incoming variation below threshold Y
```

Forbidden adapter output:

```text
this action is optimal
this branch should win
this move is the shortest route using hidden map
this choice has the best future value
```

## Consciousness bridge caution
The burden/tension account may later support a theory of meaning or consciousness by making relational salience partially objectifiable: a private state can be described by what it pressures, what it makes salient, what it resists, what it preserves, and what collapse it demands. This is not yet a proof of consciousness. It is a bridge from private-seeming content to relational-transformational structure.

## Status
This derivation closes the working conceptual definition of burden for kernel purposes. It does not yet close:

- a full formal algebra of burden operations;
- exact quotient tolerance;
- numerical burden estimation;
- consciousness/felt-relevance theory;
- runtime implementation.
