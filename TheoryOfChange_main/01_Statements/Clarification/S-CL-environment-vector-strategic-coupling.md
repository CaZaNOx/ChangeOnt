# Strategic coupling states how much valid support depends on other optimizing or reactive agents/processes
## Content
Strategic coupling measures how much the validity of current continuation depends on other agents or reactive processes optimizing with or against the focal system. This is not mere multiplicity of entities; it is the extent to which continuation quality is co-determined by adaptive opposition or cooperation.

## Why this vector is needed
Without a global vector for strategic coupling, fixed-rule games and fixed-rule mazes can be placed too close even though the former contain adaptive opposition that changes what current support means.

## Kernel relationship
High strategic coupling lowers naive trust in carry-forward, raises the legitimacy of broader rival maintenance, and reduces how directly local support can be treated as exogenous. Low strategic coupling allows more world-like rather than opponent-reactive stabilization.

## Placement guidance
Ask: How much can another agent or reactive process invalidate current support by adapting? How much of the objective depends on anticipating counter-moves, opponent responses, or reactive co-determination?

## Directional law
Adding an adaptive opponent, making another process strategically responsive, or increasing adversarial optimization should raise this vector. Removing opponent adaptation should lower it.

## Misuse warning
This vector is global only if it is measured by the same admissible logic across problems. It must not be replaced by family labels or by hidden oracle information. If a problem makes this vector hard to estimate, the right output is a wider region or lower confidence—not a family-specific guess.
