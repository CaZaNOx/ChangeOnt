# Pressure-signature continuation branch identity

## Claim
A continuation branch is individuated by a retained continuation-pressure signature, not by the native action through which it is currently expressed.

A pressure signature is the minimally retained profile of unresolved continuation pressure that keeps a possible unfolding answerable as the same branch across local transformations. It includes, when operative:

- task-anchor relation;
- carried or emerging remaining transformation burden;
- unresolved transformation direction;
- retained invariant residue;
- relation to neighboring continuations;
- admissibility constraints;
- collapse condition.

A branch exists only while this profile still makes an operative difference to admissible continuation. When the differences no longer matter under the active local closure, the branch may thin, quotient, or collapse.

## Philosophical translation
A branch is the “same way of continuing” only because a pressure profile persists through change. The same action can express different branches, and different actions can express one branch, because identity belongs to the continuity of unresolved pressure, not to the interface token.

For example, `RUN` can express stable operation, hidden-risk postponement, repair-test, or debt-carrying continuation. The action label alone does not tell us which continuation pressure is being carried.

## Why this follows from the chain
The core chain already establishes that identity is not frozen sameness. Identity is continuation-through-change under retained invariants. Remaining transformation burden then adds that a continuation can remain locally supported while still owing deformation. Minimal adequate retention requires retaining enough of this unresolved burden and invariant residue to prevent premature collapse. Thin collapse law forbids replacing that live pressure profile with a thinner action label while operative differences remain.

Therefore, a branch is not the local action. It is the pressure-bearing continuation identity that may be locally expressed through one or more actions.

## Critical archive residue
The early AI archive is not treated as authority. It contains self-report, overstatement, and simulated-read failure modes. Its useful residue is narrower: several exchanges suggest that recursive identity can be better described as continuous modulation under pressure than as possession of a stable self-token.

This supports, but does not prove, the present structural claim: what persists is a deformation/pressure profile, not a label. The archive does not justify a consciousness claim. It only sharpens the branch-identity concept already derivable from `_main`.

## Formal sketch
```text
Let C be a possible continuation inside a bounded continuation-profile P.
Let S(C) be the pressure signature of C:

S(C) = <anchor(C), burden(C), direction(C), invariant(C), relations(C), admissibility(C), collapse_condition(C)>

A branch Br_i exists when:
1. C_i remains admissible under P;
2. S(C_i) is locally distinguishable from other live signatures;
3. differences in S(C_i) remain operative for burden, relation, quotient, recursion, or collapse;
4. collapsing C_i to action/state label would erase operative structure.

Two local expressions e_a and e_b belong to the same branch when their pressure signatures remain equivalent under active continuation tolerance.

One expression e may express different branches when it is compatible with distinct non-equivalent pressure signatures.
```

## Consequences
1. Branch identity must outrank action labels in any continuation-field runtime.
2. A code path in which branch identity defaults to action labels is provisional, not a full implementation of the continuation-field doctrine.
3. Relation publication must operate over pressure signatures, not bare action names.
4. Quotienting merges pressure signatures whose remaining differences no longer change admissible continuation.
5. Grey preservation retains non-equivalent pressure signatures; it is not mere indecision.

## Anti-smuggling boundary
A pressure signature may not be derived from hidden optimality or reward ranking. It must be traceable to public or parity-honest facts: observable history, declared transition grammar, public burden/effect facts, legal constraints, kernel-side history, or admissible relation topology.

Forbidden pressure signatures include:

- “best current action,”
- “optimal replacement threshold,”
- “shortest hidden path,”
- “DP-preferred branch,”
- any identity derived from hidden simulator state or benchmark outcome.
