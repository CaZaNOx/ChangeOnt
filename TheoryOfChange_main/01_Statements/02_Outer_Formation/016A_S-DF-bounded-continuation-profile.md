# Bounded continuation-profile — what later continuation remains answerable to

## Claim (formal)
Before full identity-claims are made, a bounded continuing line can acquire a continuation-profile: a recurrently reinforced pattern of admissible continuation to which later unfolding can remain more or less answerable.

## Philosophical Translation (of formal claim)
What later survives is not a frozen object and not a single stored mark. What first survives is a way of continuing: a bounded profile of what tends to recur, what can vary, and what still counts as continuation of this line rather than some merely neighboring one.

## Why this claim is needed
Selective recurrence and admissibility make it plausible that some local lines continue more stably than others. But identity-language still risks arriving too quickly unless the chain specifies what later continuation is answerable to. This file secures that missing middle: before identity is recognized, there is already a bounded continuation-profile.

## Philosophical Justification
[[01_Statements/02_Outer_Formation/009_S-DF-bounded-local-hold.md]] yields a bounded local line rather than a ready-made object. [[01_Statements/02_Outer_Formation/010_S-DF-selective-recurrence.md]] yields repeatable re-entry rather than one-off carry. [[01_Statements/02_Outer_Formation/012_S-DF-continuation-admissibility.md]] distinguishes supportable from broken continuation, and [[01_Statements/02_Outer_Formation/013_S-DF-local-comparability-field.md]] makes graded retained-versus-altered continuation explicit. Together these earn a weaker survivor than static sameness: a profile of continuation that later unfolding may preserve, strain, or break.

## Explanation (informal)
A continuation-profile is the recurrent local way a line keeps going. It is not one frozen content-token. It is a bounded pattern of retention, tolerance, and recurrent bias that later unfolding can remain answerable to.

## Derivation (Philosophical)
- A bounded local line can recur selectively rather than reseed arbitrarily.
- Such recurrence can remain more or less admissible under transformation.
- Local comparability makes preserved-versus-altered continuation gradable.
- Therefore a bounded line can acquire a characteristic continuation-profile to which later unfolding is more or less answerable.

## Derivation (Formal/Logical/Mathematical)
```text
Let L be a bounded local line.
Let Rec(L) be its recurrent carry-forward.
Let A_cont(L) be the admissible continuation region.
Let C*(L_t, L_t') grade preserved-versus-altered continuation.

Then Prof(L) is the bounded continuation-profile induced by
recurrent admissible continuation under graded comparability.
```

## Clarifications / Further Context
- This file does not yet claim full identity.
- A continuation-profile is weaker than a recognized same-thing judgment.
- It is also weaker than later invariant-content; it names the bounded way a line continues before preserved content is sharply extracted.
- Later identity claims become answerability claims relative to such a profile.

## Objection 1: “Why not just call this identity already?”
Because the route has not yet earned the sharper claim that the line should count as one continuing identity rather than only a recurrent bounded profile. Identity comes next.

## Objection 2: “Is this just similarity in advance?”
No. Similarity compares transformed lines. A continuation-profile is what there is to compare them against in the first place: the recurrent bounded way this line continues.

## What this file establishes
This file establishes:
1. a non-static survivor earlier than identity,
2. the idea that later continuation can be answerable to a bounded local profile,
3. the missing bridge between recurrence/admissibility and identity-through-change.

## What this file does not yet establish
It does **not** yet establish:
- a final same/not-same verdict,
- identity-recognition,
- or preserved invariant-content in the later stronger sense.
