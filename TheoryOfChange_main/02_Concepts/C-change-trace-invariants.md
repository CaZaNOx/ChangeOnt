# Change, local shape-sensitive continuation, residue, trace, and invariant regimes
Overview:
This concept page gathers the outer bridge from continuous/processive present change to locally persistent structured regimes. The bridge now centers not on memory-like storage but on **shape-sensitive continuation**: local reshaping forms local shape, continuation occurs through that shape rather than beside it, some differentiations matter more than others, and some of those can later matter again as minimal trace. From there the route proceeds to selective recurrence, invariant regimes, continuation-admissibility, burden, operative relevance, regime signatures, and representation discipline.

What this concept resolves:
- why continuity alone is too weak and shape-sensitive carried constraint is needed before non-total-erasure
- why minimal trace is not primitive but is a later threshold of re-enterable differentiated shaping
- why stable identity in change is approached through selective recurrence and invariant regimes rather than imported as static essence
- why continuation-admissibility must be explicit before the identity-specific admissibility branch
- why operative relevance is needed to distinguish idle variation from continuation-critical structure
- why representation richness and thin collapse / classical-degenerate simplification must be regime-sensitive rather than arbitrary
- why local shape should now be read as a derived relational-gauge/coarseness organization, not as a primitive metaphor
- why the kernel map needs this bridge to avoid looking arbitrary
