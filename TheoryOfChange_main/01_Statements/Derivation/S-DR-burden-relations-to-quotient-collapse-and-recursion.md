# S-DR: Burden Relations to Quotient, Grey, Recursion, and Earned Collapse

Status: second-pass conceptual derivation; kernel-contract precursor; no runtime implementation claim.

This derivation continues:

```text
S-DR-burden-as-anchored-operative-tension.md
S-CL-burden-operation-table-and-stress-tests.md
S-CL-structural-proximity-path-density-and-recursion-demand.md
S-CL-grey-preservation-vs-indecision.md
S-CL-scalarization-as-thin-collapse.md
```

It answers the next conceptual question: once burden is understood as continuation-relevant de-centering in a changing relational field, how do quotient, grey preservation, recursion demand, and earned collapse follow without becoming ordinary scoring or search?

---

## 1. Starting point

Burden is not a scalar cost. Burden is anchored operative tension:

```text
burden = the continuation-relevant de-centering of a retained structure in a changing relational field
```

A burden becomes kernel-relevant only when it changes at least one of:

```text
continuation admissibility
branch identity
relation topology
quotient/equivalence status
grey-preservation need
recursion demand
collapse-readiness
```

The field cannot treat every tension as burden. A tension that does not couple to any continuation condition remains background or nonoperative at the active scale.

---

## 2. From burden operations to relations

Branch relations are not primitive labels added to candidates. They are derived from how continuations transform one another's burden, admissibility, identity, evidence, or collapse condition.

The minimal coupling basis is:

```text
burden coupling:
  A changes burden carried by B.

admissibility coupling:
  A changes whether B remains live/admissible.

evidence coupling:
  A changes what is exposed, hidden, or knowable for B.

identity coupling:
  A changes whether B remains the same branch.

collapse coupling:
  A changes whether B may collapse, must remain grey, or may quotient.
```

Named relations are derived cases:

```text
relief:
  burden coupling that lowers same-type operative burden.

cancellation:
  admissibility/condition coupling that removes the condition under which a burden remains active.

rivalry:
  admissibility coupling where A and B cannot both remain live under the same commitment scope.

dependency:
  admissibility or evidence coupling where B's status depends on A.

shared evidence:
  evidence coupling through the same unresolved source.

proximity:
  any coupling strong enough that a deformation in A changes the continuation-status of B.

quotient/equivalence:
  identity/collapse coupling where remaining differences stop mattering for active continuation.
```

This prevents relation labels from becoming arbitrary. If a proposed relation cannot be traced to one of these couplings, it is not yet a lawful kernel relation.

---

## 3. Quotient as loss of operative difference

Two branches do not quotient because they look similar. They quotient when their remaining difference no longer changes active continuation structure.

A quotient relation is justified when, under the active task-anchor and local tolerance, branches share the same operative status with respect to:

```text
burden profile
admissible transformations
relation topology
recursion demand
collapse consequences
```

Therefore:

```text
dense equivalent paths
→ quotient / merge / thin
```

not:

```text
dense equivalent paths
→ more recursion merely because there are many paths
```

Quotient is an earned thinning of difference. It is not a representational convenience unless the lost difference is nonoperative for continuation.

---

## 4. Grey preservation as retention of operative difference

Grey preservation is not indecision. It is the refusal to collapse live differences that still affect continuation.

Grey must be preserved when at least one unresolved branch difference still changes:

```text
burden status
admissibility
relation topology
quotient/equivalence
recursion demand
collapse consequence
```

Therefore:

```text
dense non-equivalent paths
→ grey preservation and/or bounded recursion
```

Grey may collapse only when retaining the difference no longer changes continuation-relevant structure.

---

## 5. Recursion demand as structure-change demand

Recursion is not search depth. Search depth asks what happens after more actions. CO recursion asks whether another layer of unfolding may change the current structure of burden, relation, quotient, grey, or collapse.

Recursion is justified when further unfolding can change at least one of:

```text
which burdens are operative
whether a burden is relieved, canceled, transformed, exposed, or masked
whether branches quotient or remain non-equivalent
whether a rival remains live
whether a collapse is earned
whether a hidden burden becomes visible
whether path density is equivalent or non-equivalent
```

Recursion is not justified merely because many actions are available. Many equivalent paths should quotient. Many irrelevant paths should thin. A sparse high-consequence unresolved branch may justify recursion even when path density is low.

---

## 6. Earned collapse as structured thinning

Collapse is earned when retaining additional live branch structure would no longer change the local continuation profile in a way relevant to:

```text
burden
admissibility
relation topology
quotient/equivalence
cancellation/relief
recursion demand
downstream commitment
```

A collapse certificate is not a single scalar threshold. A scalar may summarize parts of the certificate, but it cannot replace the certificate.

A first conceptual collapse certificate requires:

```text
1. A candidate continuation identity remains stable enough to express.
2. Its burdens are low, falling, relieved, canceled, buffered, or safely bounded.
3. Live rivals are quotient-equivalent, canceled, dependent, dominated by relation structure, or nonoperative.
4. Hiddenness is within the active regime tolerance.
5. Relation topology no longer demands another recursion layer.
6. Preserving grey would no longer change continuation-relevant structure.
7. No evidence-bearing fallback or hidden policy supplied the apparent certainty.
```

This differs from confidence thresholding because the certificate depends on relational status and burden transformation, not just score magnitude.

---

## 7. Diagnostic consequences

The derivation yields direct diagnostic cases before broad benchmarks:

```text
same scalar scores + different relation topology
→ different collapse/grey/recursion behavior
```

```text
dense equivalent branches
→ quotient rather than recursion explosion
```

```text
dense non-equivalent branches
→ grey preservation or bounded recursion
```

```text
high local support + masking/postponed burden
→ collapse delay or burden-tracking requirement
```

```text
low immediate support + explicit relief/cancellation relation
→ possible viability increase or collapse delay of burdened rival
```

If these cases fail, RCF remains action scoring with extra terminology.

## 8. Remaining open parts

This derivation does not finish:

```text
exact quotient tolerance computation
minimal relation algebra proof
full recursion scheduler
formula-level scalar grounding
comparison with known algorithms
```

It does close the conceptual bridge from burden operations to the next kernel operations: relation, quotient, grey, recursion, and earned collapse.
