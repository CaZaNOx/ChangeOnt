# d — Derived comparability distance

Definition (formal):
A shorthand for a strengthened local comparability measure after directional preservation/burden relations have already been earned. It is not a primitive background metric and should be read as a derived packaging of localized transformation cost or burden.

## Example
- In metric-like strengthening notes, `d(x,y)` abbreviates an earned local burden/comparability quantity rather than a pre-given Euclidean distance.

## Related
- [[01_Statements/SYMBOLS/Tau]] (τ), [[01_Statements/SYMBOLS/Approx]] (≈), [[01_Statements/SYMBOLS/Theta]] (θ)

## Clarifications
- Use only after local comparability has been established.
- Do not read `d` as a primitive neutral geometry.
- Where directionality matters, bend/burden notation remains more faithful than symmetric distance notation.
