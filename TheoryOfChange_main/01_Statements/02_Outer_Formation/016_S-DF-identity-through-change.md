# Identity through change — later stability over a bounded continuing line
## Claim (formal)
Identity is not primitive stasis. It is the later stability of a bounded continuing line whose later unfolding remains answerable enough to its bounded continuation-profile that it can still be treated as one continuing line through transformation.

## Philosophical Translation (of formal claim)
To stay “the same” in a change-first ontology is not to stop changing. It is for a bounded line of unfolding to continue in a way that still answers to its own recurrent profile strongly enough that it remains one line rather than a merely neighboring succession.

## Philosophical Justification
[[015_S-DR-boundary-before-identity]] already blocks objecthood from appearing too early. [[016A_S-DF-bounded-continuation-profile]] secures the weaker survivor that identity must answer to: not a frozen thing, but a recurrent bounded profile of continuation. Identity is therefore not a primitive token and not mere loose succession. It is the stronger claim that later transformation remains answerable enough to that same bounded profile that the line still counts as one continuing line.

## Explanation (informal)
Identity through change is weaker than full recognition or thresholded sameness. It says only that a bounded line remains stable enough, and remains answerable enough to its own continuing profile, to count as continuing rather than broken into a merely related successor.

## Derivation (Philosophical)
- Boundaries come before identity.
- A bounded local line can acquire a recurrent continuation-profile.
- Later unfolding can remain more or less answerable to that profile under transformation.
- Where that answerability remains strong enough, one continuing line persists through change.
- Therefore identity is a later claim about bounded profile-answerable continuation, not static equality.

## Derivation (Formal/Logical/Mathematical)
```text
IdentityThroughChange(L) := L is a bounded continuing line whose later transformations
remain admissibly answerable to Prof(L) strongly enough that L is still treated as one continuing line.
```

## Clarifications / Further Context
- This node is earlier than thresholded identity-recognition.
- Similarity operators and invariants come later as more explicit tests.
- Identity here is neither static sameness nor mere lineage; it is bounded same-line answerability through change.
