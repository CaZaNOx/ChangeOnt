# Self-similarity threshold — sharpened recognition over admissibility
## Claim (formal)
A self-similarity threshold `θ` is a derivative sharpening imposed over an existing identity-admissibility field when a context requires a more decisive recognition rule.

## Philosophical Translation (of formal claim)
Threshold is not the first form of identity. It is a later collapse of a graded admissibility field into a stronger same-enough decision where action or reporting demands it.

## Philosophical Justification
[[01_Statements/02_Outer_Formation/023_S-DF-similarity-operator]] gives the graded retained-versus-altered comparison and [[01_Statements/02_Outer_Formation/024_S-DF-identity-admissibility]] gives the weaker tolerance field. Only after both are available does threshold become meaningful. Threshold therefore does not create identity; it sharpens already-earned admissible continuity.

## Explanation (informal)
Thresholding draws a firmer line across a field that was already there. It is derivative and context-sensitive.

## Derivation (Philosophical)
- Similarity provides graded comparison.
- Identity admissibility provides the broader field of tolerated continuity.
- Some contexts require stronger same-enough cuts for intervention, attribution, or reporting.
- Therefore thresholded recognition is a later sharpening of admissibility rather than its source.

## Derivation (Formal/Logical/Mathematical)
```text
Given A_id(P,K; C),
thresholded recognition obtains when a context-specific surface θ sharpens membership conditions inside A_id.
```

## Clarifications / Further Context
- Threshold remains derivative from admissibility.
- Thresholds can vary by regime, task, and scale.
- This node is still about recognition, not about what content is preserved.

## Next Steps in Chain

- then distinguish that recognition structure from the invariant-content it later supports.
