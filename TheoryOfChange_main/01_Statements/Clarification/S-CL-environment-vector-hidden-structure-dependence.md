# Hidden-structure dependence states how much correct unfolding depends on currently unseen structure
## Content
Hidden-structure dependence measures how much the correctness of present continuation depends on information that is not yet available in current admissible evidence. This is not identical to low coverage. A problem may have incomplete coverage yet still permit robust continuation because the hidden part barely matters. Conversely, a problem may have moderate missing coverage but be highly dependent on exactly what is hidden.

## Why this vector is needed
Coverage alone is too coarse. Dynamic partial maze exposed this directly: not all missing information matters equally, and the kernel must know when hidden structure has high authority over current choice.

## Kernel relationship
High hidden-structure dependence weakens the legitimacy of local lock-in, increases the need for broader rival maintenance, and raises the legitimacy of information-seeking or more conservative stabilization. Low hidden-structure dependence allows current support to act with more authority even if the whole space is not yet fully revealed.

## Placement guidance
Ask: If currently hidden structure were varied across plausible lawful completions, how often would the best current continuation change? How much of the decision depends on unseen topology, unseen state, or unresolved latent structure rather than already available support?

## Directional law
If walls move behind occlusion more often, if hidden cards matter more, or if latent state becomes more decisive, hidden-structure dependence should rise. If the hidden portion becomes less relevant to current choice, it should fall.

## Misuse warning
This vector is global only if it is measured by the same admissible logic across problems. It must not be replaced by family labels or by hidden oracle information. If a problem makes this vector hard to estimate, the right output is a wider region or lower confidence—not a family-specific guess.
