# Scaffold stability states how stable the carrier structure and invariant support-bearing background are
## Content
Scaffold stability measures how stable the carrier constraints or invariant support-bearing structures are. This is the stable-background side of persistence. It concerns the durability of the framework within which payloads, local identities, or current supports are attached.

## Why this vector is needed
A kernel must distinguish between a world where the very carrier structure drifts and a world where the carrier persists while only attached content changes. Renewal showed that collapsing these into one persistence term is too coarse.

## Kernel relationship
High scaffold stability legitimizes structure-level carry-forward, re-identification, and selective retention of background constraints. Low scaffold stability weakens those permissions and makes even carrier-level persistence provisional.

## Placement guidance
Ask: Are the relevant rules, legalities, topological constraints, arm identities, phase scaffold, or background invariants stable across the active horizon? Do contradictions primarily attack the scaffold itself or only current payloads on it?

## Directional law
Making rules, legality, topology, or carrier identities drift should lower scaffold stability. Holding them fixed while other content changes should keep it high.

## Misuse warning
This vector is global only if it is measured by the same admissible logic across problems. It must not be replaced by family labels or by hidden oracle information. If a problem makes this vector hard to estimate, the right output is a wider region or lower confidence—not a family-specific guess.
