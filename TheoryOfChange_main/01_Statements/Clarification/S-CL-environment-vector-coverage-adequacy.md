# Coverage adequacy states how much admissible evidence is available for reliable continuation
## Content
Coverage adequacy measures how adequate currently admissible evidence is for supporting reliable continuation, stabilization, and revision. It is not "how much data exists in the universe" but how much task-relevant structure is presently available to the kernel through lawful observation, discovered structure, and admissible rollout. High coverage means hardening can be justified more easily because the kernel is not flying blind. Low coverage means many currently plausible lines may still be overturned by as-yet-unseen structure, so provisionality and broader rival maintenance remain more legitimate.

## Why this vector is needed
A kernel that unfolds change without knowing whether the currently visible region is adequate will mistake ignorance for stability. Coverage adequacy therefore tells the kernel whether apparent coherence is earned or merely underexposed.

## Kernel relationship
Higher coverage strengthens identity formation, local support publication, and thin collapse. Lower coverage weakens early hardening, preserves rivals longer, and raises the legitimacy of information-seeking or broader unfolding before collapse.

## Placement guidance
Ask: How much task-relevant structure remains hidden or untested relative to the current decision? How often do additional lawful observations overturn current support? What fraction of the support-bearing region is discovered rather than merely assumed?

## Directional law
If a problem is made more partially observable, more occluded, or more under-sampled, coverage adequacy should not increase. If more lawful evidence becomes available without changing the underlying task, coverage adequacy should not decrease.

## Misuse warning
This vector is global only if it is measured by the same admissible logic across problems. It must not be replaced by family labels or by hidden oracle information. If a problem makes this vector hard to estimate, the right output is a wider region or lower confidence—not a family-specific guess.
