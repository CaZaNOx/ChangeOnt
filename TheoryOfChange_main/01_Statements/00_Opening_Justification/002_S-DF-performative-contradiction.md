# Performative contradiction — a denial can fail in the very act that enacts it

## Claim
If the act of asserting, denying, doubting, or bracketing `¬P` already relies on, reinstates, or enacts `P`, then the denial is **performatively unstable**. In that case `¬P` is not yet available as a responsible opening denial, even if wider later theory may still reinterpret what `P` ultimately means.

## What this means precisely
A performative contradiction is not merely an inconsistency between two sentences. It is a mismatch between:
- what is said or denied, and
- what the act of saying or denying already does.

This matters at the opening because the project is not yet allowed to borrow a thick theory of reality in order to decide what may be denied. It therefore needs a thinner test available from within inquiry itself.

So the point is not:
- that performative contradiction settles every metaphysical dispute,
- or that whatever is performatively unavoidable is already a full ontology.

The point is narrower:
- if one cannot deny something without relying on it in the act of denial,
- then one has not yet earned the right to remove it from the opening.

## Why this matters in a foundational opening
A foundational inquiry cannot begin by helping itself to whatever background theory it later hopes to justify. It therefore needs an opening test that is:
- thinner than a full ontology,
- thinner than a complete logic,
- but still strong enough to distinguish responsible exclusion from self-undermining exclusion.

Performative instability matters because it does exactly that.
It asks:
- what must already be in play for this denial to be enacted at all?

If the denial depends on what it rejects, then the opening has learned something important: the denied content may still later be re-described, weakened, or reinterpreted, but it is not removable *at the opening*.

## Core argument
1. The opening seeks what can be responsibly bracketed without collapsing inquiry.
2. A denial counts as responsible only if the act of making it can be carried out coherently.
3. Some denials fail this test because the act of making them already depends on what is denied.
4. In such cases the denial is performatively unstable.
5. Therefore performative instability is a legitimate opening test for whether a candidate commitment may be removed.

## Minimal formal schema
```text
If Enact(¬P) ⇒ Requires(P),
then ¬P is performatively unstable at the opening.
```

This does **not** yet mean:
```text
P is the whole ontology.
```
It means only:
```text
P is not yet responsibly excludable at the root.
```

## Simple examples of the form
The point is easiest to see in schematic examples.

- If I deny that any assertion is occurring while asserting, the denial enacts what it rejects.
- If I deny that any inquiry is occurring while conducting inquiry, the denial again reinstates what it denies.

The opening will later ask whether denial of happening/change has this same form.

## Why this is not a cheap trick
A common complaint is that performative contradiction is rhetorical cleverness. But the present use is more disciplined than that.

The opening does not say:
- “whatever sounds self-referential wins.”

It says:
- when the act of denial itself must rely on what is denied,
- the denial cannot function as an admissible starting elimination.

That is not rhetoric. It is a constraint on what may be removed without destroying the very exercise of removal.

## Objections and replies
### Objection 1: “A performative contradiction need not prove falsity.”
Correct. This file does not claim otherwise. It proves only opening inadmissibility of the denial, not the full final truth of the denied content in all later formulations.

### Objection 2: “Isn’t this already a lot of logic?”
No. The file uses only the thin distinction between:
- enacting a denial,
- and the conditions that enactment already presupposes.
A full formal system is not being imported here.

### Objection 3: “Couldn’t later theory reinterpret what was performatively secured?”
Yes. That is why this file speaks of opening admissibility, not final doctrinal closure. The opening secures a foothold, not yet the whole finished metaphysics.

## What this file establishes
- Performative instability is a legitimate opening test.
- It can show that some denials are unavailable at the root.
- It gives the opening a thinner criterion than a full inherited ontology.

## What this file does not yet establish
- that change is already the final primitive,
- that every performatively unavoidable element is thereby fully understood,
- or that performative contradiction alone yields the entire chain.
