# Metric-like comparability strengthening from the local comparability field
## Claim (formal)
Local reach together with graded similarity/comparability yields a distance-like comparison field — at first only quasi-metric or cost-like in force — from which stronger metric behavior may later emerge as a collapse case if additional symmetry/separation conditions are justified.

## Philosophical Translation (of formal claim)
From localized neighborhoods plus preserved-vs-altered comparison, we can derive something distance-like. But what appears first is not necessarily a full classical metric; it is a comparability or cost field that may later collapse toward metric behavior in more rigid regimes.

## Philosophical Justification
[[01_Statements/02_Outer_Formation/007_S-DF-localreach-zone]] gives locality and neighborhood before a finished geometry. [[01_Statements/02_Outer_Formation/023_S-DF-similarity-operator]] gives graded comparison of retained versus altered aspect. Together they justify saying that some transformations are easier, harder, nearer, or farther in a disciplined sense. What is earned first is therefore a comparability/cost structure, not automatically a full symmetric metric space.

## Explanation (informal)
If local states can reach one another and can be compared by how much preservation versus alteration they involve, then we can speak of distance-like burden or cost. This may behave like a quasi-metric, pseudometric, or local cost field. Only later, if symmetry and stronger identity conditions hold, does a full metric become justified.

## Derivation (Philosophical)
- Reach/locality provides neighborhoods and directed relatedness.
- Similarity provides graded preservation/alteration comparison.
- Combining these yields a disciplined notion of comparative burden, loss, or distance-likeness.
- Full metric language is therefore derivative and conditional, not primitive.

## Derivation (Formal/Logical/Mathematical)
```text
Given local reach R and graded comparison Sim,
one may define a distance-like comparison field d* whose values track transformation burden or preservation loss.
At first, d* need satisfy only local comparability or quasi-metric style laws; stronger metric axioms require later justification.
```

## Clarifications / Further Context
- This node does not claim a full Euclidean or symmetric metric as primitive.
- The first earned object is a comparability/cost field.
- Symmetry, identity-of-indiscernibles, and strong triangle laws may arise only in stabilized collapse regimes.
- Bend and gauge may later refine this field into more structured deformation geometry.

## Active-chain status
**Status band:** derived-but-weaker  
**Reason:** the chain earns a distance-like comparability field clearly; full metric strength remains a later strengthening rather than a primitive commitment.
